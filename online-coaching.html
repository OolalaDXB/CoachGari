The problem with a training plan is never the plan

Almost everyone who has tried online coaching has a document somewhere with twelve weeks in it. It was probably a reasonable document. It stopped in week three, and not because the exercises were wrong — because nobody noticed it had stopped.

So this is built the other way round. The plan matters less than the fact that we speak, that I can see how you are moving, and that when something is not working it changes that week instead of at the end of the block.

How it works

  • We meet on videoTwice a month, in real time, to touch base and go through what has actually happened rather than what was supposed to.
  • Works with what you haveYour plan is built around your reality — a gym, a home, or simply outdoors. The space decides the programme, not the other way round.
  • Meal planningA simple shift in meal prepping and shopping habits, based on where you live and what is on the shelves there.
  • Monitored in betweenPushed and encouraged in real time, which is the part a document cannot do and the reason most documents fail.

Who it suits

People training at home, in a local gym, or wherever they can find a floor. People in a city with no coach worth the money. People who travel enough that a fixed gym schedule was never going to survive. And people who have done this alone, got results, and then lost them — usually around the time nobody was watching any more.

What it is not

It is not a subscription to a library of videos. It is not a spreadsheet emailed once. And it is not a programme that assumes you have a squat rack, a full night's sleep and ninety free minutes, because most people have none of the three.

When the session is the conversation

Not every call has to end in a workout. Some people book an hour just to talk it through: what is blocking them, why the last three attempts stopped, what they actually want out of this. It is a session in its own right, and it works the same whether you are down the road or six time zones away.
