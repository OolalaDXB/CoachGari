Padel punishes the player who is late, not the player who is weak

Most people who book a padel session think they need a better shot. Usually they need to be somewhere else, half a second earlier. Padel is played in a small box with walls, which means the point is decided less by how hard you hit and more by where you were standing when the ball arrived — and whether you and your partner were standing there together.

That is what these sessions work on first. Not because technique does not matter, but because a better backhand played from the wrong position is still a lost point, and because position is the part that improves fastest.

What a session covers

  • MovementHow you get to the ball and how you recover from it. Split step, first step, the small adjustments that decide whether you arrive balanced or reaching.
  • Court positionWhere to be at the net, when to hold the line, who takes the middle, and how a pair moves as one unit rather than two players covering their own half.
  • Shot selectionWhich ball to play, and — more often the useful lesson — which ball not to. Reading the wall, using the lob, knowing when the point is not yours to win yet.
  • Match playPlaying the pattern rather than the shot: building a point, recognising when you have the advantage, and what to do in the three seconds after you lose it.

Levels

Sessions are shaped around your level and what you want to improve, so there is no minimum. A first session with someone who has never held a padel racket looks nothing like a session with a player who competes and keeps losing the same kind of point — and both are normal.

If you have played tennis, expect to spend the first session unlearning something. The grip transfers, the instinct to step back from the wall does not.

One-to-one, or with your partner

Both exist. For padel specifically, coaching a pair is often the better use of the hour: most of what decides a point is the relationship between two players, and that is difficult to coach one player at a time. If you play regularly with the same partner, bring them.

Small groups also run. Group size is kept small enough that everyone hits; ask when you enquire and you will be told the number for that session.

Where

In person in Dubai. Courts are arranged when the session is booked — tell me where you normally play and we will start there.

And online, for the part that travels

The striking needs a court. The thinking does not. Match footage can be reviewed on a call, and that is frequently where the pattern shows up: the same position lost, the same shot chosen under pressure, the same two seconds where the pair drifted apart. It is the cheapest hour of padel coaching you will ever buy, and it works from anywhere.

Questions

Do I need to have played padel before?

No. Sessions are shaped around your level and what you want to improve, so a first session can start with how to hold the racket and where to stand — or with why you keep losing the point after a good serve.

How long is a padel session?

Sixty minutes on court, whether one-to-one or in a small group.

Can you coach a pair rather than one player?

Yes, and for padel it is often the better choice. Most of what decides a padel point is the relationship between two players — who takes the middle, who covers the lob, when you both move forward. That is hard to coach one player at a time.

Do you coach padel online?

Not the striking, which needs a court. But match footage can be reviewed on a call, and that is often where the pattern shows.

What does a padel session cost?

Price is given on request, because it depends on whether the session is one-to-one or a group, and on the court. Ask through the booking page and you will have a figure before anything is booked.

Do I need my own racket?

Bring one if you have one. If you do not, say so when you book and it will be sorted before you arrive.
